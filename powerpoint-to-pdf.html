Understanding Aspect Ratios

PowerPoint presentations use different aspect ratios depending on when they were created and their intended use:

📺 16:9 Widescreen (Modern Standard)

Dimensions: 13.33 × 7.5 inches (33.87 × 19.05 cm)

Used for: Modern presentations (PowerPoint 2013+), conference talks, webinars, digital displays

Best practices: Default for new presentations. Matches modern screens and projectors.

📺 4:3 Standard (Legacy Format)

Dimensions: 10 × 7.5 inches (25.4 × 19.05 cm)

Used for: Older presentations (PowerPoint 2003 and earlier), some projectors, printed handouts

Best practices: Still used in some corporate environments and for print-focused presentations.

📐 Custom Aspect Ratios

Examples: Square (1:1), ultrawide (21:9), portrait (9:16 for mobile)

Used for: Social media content, digital signage, mobile-first presentations

Best practices: TronoPDF automatically detects and preserves any custom dimensions.

💡 Pro tip: Always check your presentation's aspect ratio before converting. A 16:9 presentation converted to 4:3 PDF will have black bars or distorted content. Our tool preserves the original ratio automatically.

Working with Slide Images

When to Use Slide Images Instead of PPTX

  • Photographed whiteboards — Convert photos of whiteboard sessions
  • Slide screenshots — Convert screenshots taken during presentations
  • Legacy formats — When you have images but not the original PPTX
  • Quick conversions — Faster than opening PowerPoint for simple needs

Best Practices for Slide Images

  • High resolution — Use at least 1920×1080 pixels for clarity
  • Consistent dimensions — All images should have same aspect ratio
  • Good lighting — Ensure text and graphics are clearly visible
  • Straight alignment — Avoid skewed or rotated photos
  • Proper order — Name files sequentially (slide-01.jpg, slide-02.jpg)

Tips for Professional PDF Output

✅ Before Conversion

  • Review in PowerPoint first — Ensure presentation looks correct
  • Check slide dimensions — Verify aspect ratio matches your needs
  • Test on different devices — Preview on mobile if important
  • Backup original — Keep the PPTX file for future edits
  • Remove hidden slides — Delete or hide slides you don't want in PDF

✅ During Conversion

  • Check slide count — Ensure all expected slides are present
  • Verify aspect ratio — Confirm slides aren't stretched or distorted
  • Test sample slides first — Use the sample feature to test quality

✅ After Conversion

  • Open and review — Check every page of the PDF
  • Test printing — Print a test page if handouts are needed

  • Add protection — Use Protect PDF for confidential content

Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Conversion Errors

  • Wrong aspect ratio — Always verify 16:9 vs 4:3 before sharing
  • Missing slides — Check slide count matches your presentation
  • Distorted content — Verify text and images aren't stretched
  • Low quality images — Ensure high-resolution source presentation

❌ Content Errors

  • Hidden slides included — Remove or hide unwanted slides first
  • Speaker notes visible — Notes don't appear in PDF (which is usually good)
  • Animation frames — Complex animations may not render perfectly
  • Embedded videos — Videos won't play in PDF format
⚠️ Important: PDF is a static format. Animations, transitions, embedded videos, and interactive elements won't work in the converted PDF. The tool captures the final visual state of each slide, which is perfect for sharing and printing, but not for interactive presentations.
