What Works Best For Conversion

✅ Ideal PDFs:

  • Text-based PDFs (created from Word, Excel, or other software)
  • PDFs with clear table structures
  • Documents with consistent formatting
  • Tables with visible borders or gridlines

❌ Challenging PDFs:

  • Scanned PDFs (images of tables) — use OCR PDF first
  • Complex layouts with merged cells
  • Tables spanning multiple pages
  • PDFs with rotated or skewed text

💡 Pro tip: If your PDF is scanned, run OCR first to make text selectable, then convert to Excel for best results.

Tips For Best Results

  • Use text-based PDFs for accurate conversion
  • Check the output — verify that columns and rows are correct
  • Clean up in Excel — fix any formatting issues manually
  • Split multi-page tables — use Split PDF first if needed
  • Keep the original PDF as reference

⚠️ Complex tables with merged cells or unusual formatting may need manual adjustment in Excel after conversion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will formulas in the PDF be preserved?

No. PDFs don't store formulas — only the calculated values. You'll need to recreate formulas in Excel.

Can I convert scanned PDFs to Excel?

Yes, but run OCR PDF first to make the text selectable, then convert.

Will merged cells be preserved?

Basic merged cells may be preserved, but complex merges might need manual adjustment in Excel.

Can I convert multiple pages at once?

Yes. All pages with tables will be converted. Each page becomes a separate sheet in Excel.
