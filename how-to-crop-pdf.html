Why Crop A PDF?

  • Remove scanner margins — Clean up dark edges from scans
  • Focus on content — Keep only the relevant part of a page
  • Reduce white space — Make documents look tighter
  • Extract sections — Isolate a table, chart, or image area
  • Prepare for printing — Remove unnecessary borders
  • Standardize pages — Make all pages the same visible area

Crop vs Rotate vs Split — Which Do You Need?

  • Crop — Remove margins/areas, keep the rest of the page
  • Rotate — Change page orientation (90°, 180°, 270°)
  • Split — Extract entire pages into separate files

For scanned documents, a common workflow is: Rotate first, then Crop to remove scanner edges, then Compress for smaller size.

Tips For Best Results

  • Leave a small margin — Don't crop too tight to the text
  • Check multiple pages — Ensure the area works for all pages
  • Apply to all pages for consistent scans
  • Keep the original — Cropping can't be undone
  • Zoom in while selecting for precision

⚠️ Cropping removes content outside the selected area permanently. Always keep a backup of the original PDF.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does cropping reduce quality?

No. Cropping only changes the visible area. The remaining content keeps its original quality.

Can I crop only some pages?

Yes. Apply the crop to all pages or select specific pages.

Can I undo a crop?

No. Cropping is permanent in the output file. Keep your original PDF as backup.

Does cropping reduce file size?

Slightly, but not significantly. Use Compress PDF for real size reduction.
